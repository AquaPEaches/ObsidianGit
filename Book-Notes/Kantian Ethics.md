## **§1* On the sources of metaphysics

> If one wishes to present a body of cognition as a science. Then one must first be able to determine precisely the *differentia* it has in common with no other science.
> 
> ~ P. 15

To say in short. Kant explains that for <mark>a **body of cognition** to presented as a science. It needs to have it's own *distinguishing feature*. </mark>



Biology, Physics, Mathematics and Chemistry are all **bodies of cognition**. What makes them their own individual body of sciences. Is that their source is uniquely based upon.



Why is this important?

- Kant argues that metaphysal cognition is it's own body of **science** with the use of **<mark>reason.</mark>**



## *§ 2* On the type of cognition that alone can be called metaphysical



### (a) On the distinctions between synthetic and analytic judgments in general

Metaphysical cognition is based upon judgements *<mark>a priori</mark>.*

A priori - independent of all exprience and sensory perception.



Their are two types of judgements:

1. **Analytic judgements:**
   
   1. One that is to <u>explain and do nothing</u> to the cognition.
2. **Synthetic Judgements:**
   1.  <u>Amplifies and or augments</u> the given cognition.



### (b) The common principle of all analytic judgements is <mark>the principle of contradiction</mark>



All analytic judgements are a priori in nature. Even if the subject that you are placing the judgement on is empirical or not. Due to analytic judgements being merely explinations of the predicates of a subject; You can't deny it from the subject without it being a contradiction.



If the judgement can be varied of a sole subject, then it is not a analytic judgement of the cognition. E.g "All apples are red", someone can argue that some apples are infact not red and are green, or even yellow without contradicting the concept of an apple. Therefore the Analytic judgement that apples are red is false and fails to pass the principle of contradiction.
