# Michel De Montaigne

> "I myself am the subject of my book" - *What Do I know?* 

**Michel Eyquem de Montaigne** was a French philospher, writer and politician during the French Renaissance. He primarily studied humanity and by extension, himself. 

**Pioneer** of the ==Essays==. He wrote until his final breath. Due to his improper style of writing, which delved into personal anecdotes and personal ruminations, it was seen as detrimental to the proper style, rather than innovation. His most know quote was "What do I know?"

*Born in 1533 and passed away in 1592*

# 1 On Sorrow

## True Pain
> "Whoever says he burns with love scarce burns at all," -Petracrch, Canzoniere, 170

Probably the most impactful quote in the chapter, Montaigne expresses that emotions too profound can never be expressed. Which is why so many people in history when hit with tragic news don't burt out in tears but are, infact, so overwhelmed with pain that their body does not know how to express it.

Which is why this quote is important. One who can express their suffering, is not in pain at all.

## Sorrow is the culmination of pain
>"..he saw his son being led away to his death, he maintained the same composure. But on observing one of his household being led away among the captives, he fell into a state of deep grief."  P.18 Psammenitus, King of Egypt

Montaigne brings forth examples of how we have a certain amount of tolerance to pain before we collapse. Be it physical, psychological or emotional. 

He brings forth stories where fathers and mothers keep their composure even as they lose their lose wars, become captives of their enemies, or even lose their children and or loved ones. Not showing a shred of emotion in their face or composure. But these same men and women, who have faced countless horrors and beared them so couragously, completely crumble when news of their maid getting captured is given or falling in grief for one of their courtiers passing away. 

It is not that the events that happened before were insignifcant. They were so signifcant infact, that the culmination of all that grief gave opportunity of even the smallest of inconveniences to completely breakdown a person.
